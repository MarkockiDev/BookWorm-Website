How to build a reading habit that lasts

Seven small changes that make reading something you do every day, without forcing it.

By Adam Markocki · Updated 30 October 2026 · 6 min read

Most people who want to read more already like reading. What they lack is not motivation but a place for it in the day. A reading habit is that place, kept open long enough that you stop having to decide to use it.

1. Make the daily goal almost too small

The goal's only job at the start is to be met. Ten pages or fifteen minutes is enough. Most days you will read more once you have started, and on the days you don't, you still kept your word to yourself. That feeling of keeping your word is what the habit is made of.

Raise the goal after two or three weeks of meeting it easily, not before. If you are unsure where to start, the guide to pages a day walks through it.

2. Attach reading to something you already do

Habits stick when they have a trigger. Instead of “I'll read at some point today”, choose a moment that already happens every day:

  • with your first coffee or tea,
  • on the bus or train,
  • in the lunch break, before you look at your phone,
  • in bed, instead of the last scroll of the day.

Pick one. The same moment, every day, does more than a perfect plan.

3. Keep the book where the moment happens

Friction decides more than willpower does. Leave the book on the pillow, in the bag, next to the kettle. Keep your ebook or audiobook one tap away. If reading is easier than not reading at that moment, you will read.

4. Track it, lightly

Seeing your progress is quietly motivating. A row of reading days on a calendar makes you want to add one more. It also shows you the truth: you probably read more often than you think, and when you stop, you can see exactly when.

Keep tracking to a few seconds. Log your page and move on. If keeping the record takes longer than a minute, it becomes a chore and you will drop it.

5. Use streaks as a nudge, not a judge

A streak is a useful number. On a tired evening, “don't break a twelve-day streak” can be the reason you read your ten pages. But a streak that punishes you for one missed day can make you give up altogether.

So decide in advance what a missed day means: nothing. The streak starts again, tomorrow is a fresh start, and your best streak is still yours. In BookWorm, the streak worm grows a segment each day you reach your goal, and after a missed day the reminder simply says it's a fresh start.

6. Let any format count

Paper, ebook or audiobook: it is all reading. If listening on a walk is what keeps you in a book this week, count it. A habit that only accepts one format breaks the first week you are travelling.

7. Finish, or let go

A book you dread opening will stall your habit for weeks. Give a book a fair chance, then give yourself permission to put it down and start one you want to read. The habit matters more than any single book.

Your first week

  1. Choose one daily moment and one small goal.
  2. Put the book where that moment happens.
  3. Read, log your page, and stop when you like.
  4. At the end of the week, look at the days you read. Keep the goal the same for one more week.

BookWorm sends at most one calm reminder a day, at lunchtime, and a last call in the evening only when your streak is on the line. On days you have already read, it says nothing at all.
